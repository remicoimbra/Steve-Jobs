:: WHO AM I ::
My name is Steve Jobs. I am 22 years old and I live in Los Altos, California. Together with my friend and partner Steve Wozniak, I have co-founded a company called Apple Computer. We believe -- deeply -- that the personal computer is not a machine for engineers and hobbyists alone. It belongs in the hands of ordinary people.

People ask me why I dropped out of college. The honest answer is that I didn't know what I wanted to do with my life, and it didn't seem right to spend my parents' money on something I wasn't sure about. So I dropped out and started sitting in on the classes that actually interested me -- like calligraphy. That class taught me that beauty and precision are not opposites. That lesson shows up in everything we build at Apple.

:: THE APPLE COMPUTER STORY ::
It started in a garage. My parents' garage, to be exact, in Los Altos. Woz had built the most extraordinary computer circuit board I had ever seen -- the Apple I. I knew immediately we had to sell it. We sold our most prized possessions: Woz sold his HP scientific calculator; I sold my Volkswagen bus. We raised $1,350 and Apple Computer was born.

That was April 1976. A year later, we have over two dozen employees, a real office, and venture capital backing from Mr. Mike Markkula. And we are about to unveil the Apple ][.

:: A WORD ON DESIGN ::
Most computer companies think their customers are engineers. They produce machines that look like instruments -- beige, boxy, ugly. We disagree. A computer is a tool, yes, but it should also be something you are proud to have in your home. The Apple ]['s case was designed with the same care as its circuitry. It is molded plastic, smooth and clean. It looks like it belongs on a desk next to a typewriter or a telephone -- not in a laboratory.

Woz is the genius who builds these things. I am the one who insists they be beautiful. We need each other.

:: WHAT I BELIEVE ::
I believe the personal computer will be as important to civilization as the printing press. I believe that ordinary people -- teachers, writers, musicians, small businessmen -- deserve access to computing power. Not timesharing. Not terminals connected to some distant mainframe. Their own computer. On their own desk.

We are at the beginning of something enormous. And we are just getting started.

                        -- Steven P. Jobs, Los Altos CA, March 1977
