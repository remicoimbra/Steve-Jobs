Building the
personal
computer era.

My name is Steve Jobs. I grew up in the Bay Area and dropped out of Reed College after six months — one of the better decisions I've made. I spent the next year auditing classes, sleeping on floors, and trying to figure out what I was meant to do.

In 1976, Steve Wozniak and I started Apple Computer out of my family's garage in Los Altos. We sold the Apple I as a kit to hobbyists. It worked. Then we asked ourselves: what if we made a computer that anyone could use? Not just engineers. Anyone.

That question is driving everything we do right now. I believe the computer is the most remarkable tool we've ever built. And it doesn't belong only to corporations and universities. It belongs in every home, on every desk.

We are very close to something. I can feel it.

Apple II

A computer
for the
rest of us.

The Apple II is not a kit. It is a finished product — a real computer in a real case, ready to use the moment you set it down on your desk. Color graphics. A built-in keyboard. Sound. Expandability through eight open slots.

Woz engineered a machine that shouldn't exist at this price. I made sure it would be something you'd be proud to own.

How I think

01

Design is not decoration.

A thing is well designed when it works exactly as it looks like it should. The surface and the function are the same conversation. If you have to read the manual, we failed.

02

Simplicity is the ultimate sophistication.

Anyone can make something complicated. It takes real work — and real courage — to strip away everything that isn't necessary. That's where good things live.

03

Taste is a discipline.

People act like taste is something you're born with. It isn't. It's built, slowly, by spending enormous amounts of time with the things you love and asking why they work.
